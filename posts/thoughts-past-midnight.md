Much has been said in the genre that can crudely be grouped as *Mental Models*. Analogies, frameworks, or ideas intended to help you make better decisions and get you what you want out of life. Often they are recycled and dare I say cliched. But some ideas made an unusual impression and I thought I'd share them. 

I'll share three ideas:

1. **Activation Energy:** There's a good chance you saw this in high school chemistry. Roughly speaking, it's the minimum energy to get the reaction started. If there's something you know you want to do but aren't, figure out a way to reduce the activation energy. A lot of advice, like *gamify*, *don't be a perfectionist*, the *2-minute rule* come down to this basic idea. And sometimes the reaction releases energy of its own, helping sustain itself. The best way to do it in my experience, is to do the task with fewer conditions, and once you start there's a good chance momentum will take you there anyway.


2. Crystal Seeding: Sometimes the solution is ready to crystallize, but it doesn't. Introducing a tiny seed crystal gives the molecules enough structure to organize around and once it starts, the growth is rapid. In essence, you want a small thing that can act as an anchor and allow for other parts to reorganize around it and consequently fall into place. Even in our lives, sometimes all the ingredients are there, maybe time, motivation, resources, information, but they don't come together effectively and life remains amorphous and slips by. A simple (singular) thing like a gym partner, weekly project meeting, title of an essay or the one sentence it should have, goes a long way to making it *all* (or most) come together.  


3. Amdahl's Law: From wikipedia: *the overall performance improvement gained by optimizing a single part of a system is limited by the fraction of time that the improved part is actually used*. This is highly intuitive and everyone already understands it or sees it immediately, but it was still a transformative experience sitting in CMSC216 and seeing such an obvious fact precisely stated. Of course. Fry the big fish, don't sweat the small stuff, don't be penny-wise and pound foolish etc. Even if you squash something that takes 1% down to 0, the maximum improvement is 1%.
